    Stay safe

    Spot a phishing link yourself

    Look-alike names

    paypa1.com is not paypal.com. Check every letter of the domain.

    Numbers instead of a name

    Links like http://192.168.1.5/login hide who really owns the page.

    The @ trick

    Anything before an @ is ignored by the browser and is often used to disguise the real site.

    Pressure and urgency

    Real banks never ask for your password or OTP through a link in a message.

    Odd endings

    Free or rarely used endings such as .xyz, .top or .tk deserve extra care.

    When unsure

    Type the site's address yourself instead of clicking the link.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    How accurate is it?

    It is a prototype. It judges mainly the website address, so it can still make mistakes. A "legitimate" result is not a guarantee that a link is safe.

    Does it open the link I paste?

    No. PhishGuard only analyzes the text of the address and never visits the page.

    What should I do if a link is flagged?

    Do not open it and do not enter any details. If it came in a message, delete it and report it as phishing in your email or messaging app.

    Can a phishing page still look safe to it?

    Yes. A fake page hosted on a trusted site can slip through, because the address itself looks normal. Always combine the result with your own care.
