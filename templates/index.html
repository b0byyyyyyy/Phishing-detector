    About PhishGuard

    PhishGuard checks a web link and tells you whether it looks like a phishing attempt or a normal site. Phishing links imitate banks, shops and login pages to steal passwords and card details.

    It is a student prototype. It gives you a quick second opinion, and it does not replace your own judgment or your browser's built-in protection.

    How it works

    1. You paste a link

      PhishGuard only reads the text of the URL. It never opens the page.

    2. It measures the link

      The app extracts 19 signals, such as URL length, number of dots and hyphens, raw IP addresses, the @ symbol, and suspicious words like "login" or "verify".

    3. The model scores it

      A Random Forest model, trained on a generated sample dataset, returns a phishing probability from 0 to 100%.

    4. You see the reasons

      The result lists the signals that raised or lowered the score, so you can judge for yourself.

    FAQ and safety tips

    How accurate is it?

    It is a prototype trained on a small generated dataset, so it will make mistakes. A "legitimate" result does not guarantee a link is safe.

    Do you store the links I scan?

    No. The link is analyzed when you press Scan and the result is shown to you. The app does not save it.

    What should I do if a link is flagged?

    Do not open it and do not enter any details. If it came in a message, delete it and report it as phishing in your email or messaging app.

    Spot a phishing link yourself

    • Check the domain name carefully. paypa1.com is not paypal.com.
    • Be wary of links that use an IP address, like http://192.168.1.5/login.
    • Anything before an @ in a link is ignored by the browser and is often used to hide the real site.
    • A real bank never asks for your password or OTP through a link in a message.
    • When unsure, type the site's address yourself instead of clicking.
